用手機錄音也能很好聽！離近一點、找對房間、別讓音量爆掉｜手機錄音小技巧

用手機錄說話、唱歌、演奏，播出來卻空空的、遠遠的、一堆雜音？不用急著買器材，改掉幾個小習慣就差很多。第一招離近一點：距離加倍聲音小約 6 分貝，房間的回音和雜音卻沒變小；說話大約放在一個手掌到一個手臂的距離，太貼近會有噗噗聲。第二招找到麥克風：底部、頂端、背面鏡頭旁都有小孔，手指和手機殼別擋住。第三招選對房間：浴室、空房間回音多，窗簾、沙發、地毯多的房間回音少，可以站在掛滿衣服的衣櫃前錄；關掉電扇、冷氣、抽油煙機。第四招音量別爆：太大聲波形被切平就是破音，事後幾乎救不回來，先試錄最大聲的段落；錄鼓、鋼琴、高音時手機反而要放遠，有些 App 可以關掉自動控制音量。第五招小心風聲：用身體或牆壁擋風，外接麥克風加毛毛防風罩（錄音師叫它「死貓」）。第六招設定和擺放：開勿擾或飛航模式、音質改成最高、手機放穩不要拿在手上。最後錄完戴耳機聽一次；想更好可以買領夾式麥克風。

Great Recordings on Your Phone: Get Closer, Pick the Right Room, and Don't Clip | Phone Recording Tips

Phone recordings of talking, singing, or playing that sound hollow, distant, and noisy? No need for new gear yet; a few habits make a big difference. Tip one, get closer: doubling the distance drops your voice about 6 dB while room echo and noise stay the same; for speech, keep the phone a hand's length to an arm's length away, but not so close that plosives pop. Tip two, find the microphone: there are holes at the bottom, top, and next to the rear camera, so keep fingers and cases off them. Tip three, choose the room: bathrooms and empty rooms are echoey, while curtains, sofas, and rugs soak up reflections; a closet full of clothes is a podcaster's secret weapon. Turn off fans, air conditioning, and range hoods. Tip four, don't clip: if it's too loud, the waveform gets cut flat and it's almost impossible to fix later, so test-record the loudest part; for drums, piano, or high singing, move the phone farther away, and some apps let you turn off automatic level control. Tip five, beware of wind: shield the phone with your body or a wall, or use a fuzzy windscreen (the "dead cat"). Tip six, settings and placement: Do Not Disturb or airplane mode, the highest audio quality setting, and a steady surface instead of your hand. Finally, listen back on headphones, and consider a clip-on lavalier mic.

視訊開會為什麼會聽到自己的回音？刺耳的「嗶——」嘯叫又是怎麼來的

線上開會、上網課，突然聽到自己的聲音慢半拍跑出來？還有全場摀耳朵的「嗶——」？回音的路線是：你的聲音傳到對方電腦，從對方喇叭放出來，又被對方麥克風收進去傳回你這邊，網路一來一回好幾百毫秒，所以聽到自己慢半拍，而且通常是對方那邊的設定造成的。延遲太久，大腦就把它當成第二個聲音（回扣先到效應），一邊講話一邊聽到慢半拍的自己還會讓人結巴。大部分時候沒有回音，是因為軟體有回音消除：電腦知道自己喇叭放了什麼，推算它進麥克風後的樣子再減掉，並且一直學習房間的回音；這項技術在 1960 年代由貝爾實驗室為越洋電話發展出來。但喇叭太大聲失真、兩邊同時講話、移動電腦時它會失手，有時壓得太用力讓聲音斷斷續續。嘯叫（feedback）則是麥克風→放大→喇叭→又進麥克風的迴圈，某個音高每繞一圈變大一點就越滾越大。同一個房間兩台電腦都開麥克風和喇叭最容易嘯叫，回音消除也沒辦法。小撇步：戴耳機（最有效）、喇叭小聲一點、麥克風別對著喇叭、同一間只開一台的聲音、不講話先靜音。

Why Do You Hear Your Own Echo in Video Calls? And Where Does That Piercing Feedback Beep Come From?

Ever hear your own voice come back a beat late in an online meeting, or a shrill beep that makes everyone cover their ears? The echo path: your voice travels to the other person's computer, plays from their speakers, gets picked up by their microphone, and comes back to you. The internet round trip takes a few hundred milliseconds, so you hear yourself late, and it's usually the other side's setup causing it. With that much delay, the brain hears a second sound (recall the precedence effect), and hearing yourself delayed can even make people stutter. Most calls have no echo thanks to echo cancellation: the computer knows what its speakers played, estimates how it will sound after reaching the microphone, and subtracts it, continuously learning the room. It was developed at Bell Labs in the 1960s for overseas phone calls. It slips up when speakers are too loud and distort, when both sides talk at once, or when you move the laptop, and sometimes it suppresses too hard and makes you sound choppy. Feedback is a loop: microphone, amplifier, speaker, back into the microphone; if a pitch grows a little each time around, it snowballs. Two computers in the same room with mics and speakers on is the classic cause, and echo cancellation can't help. Tips: wear headphones (most effective), turn speakers down, don't aim the mic at the speakers, use only one computer's audio per room, and mute when not talking.

耳機聽跟喇叭聽，哪裡不一樣？串音、交叉音消除與甜蜜點｜空間聲音系列⑤

空間聲音系列第五集！同一首歌，戴耳機聽常覺得聲音在腦袋裡，開喇叭聽聲音在前面的空間，差在哪裡？耳機的左右聲道各進各的耳朵；喇叭的聲音除了進同一邊的耳朵，還會繞過頭跑進另一隻耳朵，這叫串音（crosstalk）。串音不一定是壞事：1931 年英國的布魯姆林（Alan Blumlein）發明現代立體聲，左右喇叭的音量差在耳朵裡混合，低音部分變成兩耳時間差，喇叭立體聲反而靠串音成立。但雙耳錄音（假人頭錄音）是照兩耳聽到的樣子錄的，要戴耳機才有頭後面、頭頂的聲音，用喇叭播會被串音打亂、全擠回兩喇叭中間。解法是交叉音消除：讓另一個喇叭送出正負顛倒、算好時間的聲音，在耳朵抵消串音，1960 年代貝爾實驗室的 Atal 與 Schroeder 做出這個方法，後來稱為 transaural。代價一：甜蜜點很小，頭往旁邊挪幾公分高音就對不準，有些系統用鏡頭追蹤頭的位置，筆電、聲霸的虛擬環繞也用類似原理。代價二：某些音高很難抵消，要用力補償，喇叭負擔大、音色可能改變，要在方向準確和音色自然之間取捨。一般立體聲喇叭也有甜蜜點：兩喇叭和頭排成等邊三角形，坐偏時近的喇叭先到，先到效應把人聲拉過去。最後比較耳機和喇叭各自的優點。

Headphones vs. Speakers: Crosstalk, Crosstalk Cancellation, and the Sweet Spot | Spatial Sound Series 5

Part five of the spatial sound series! The same song can feel like it's inside your head on headphones but out in the room on speakers. Why? With headphones, each channel goes only into its own ear. With speakers, each speaker's sound also wraps around your head into the other ear: that's crosstalk. Crosstalk isn't always bad. Alan Blumlein's 1931 stereo invention relies on it: level differences between two speakers mix at the ears and become interaural time differences at low frequencies. But binaural (dummy head) recordings capture exactly what each ear hears, so on speakers, crosstalk scrambles the cues and everything collapses between the speakers. The fix is crosstalk cancellation: the other speaker sends a polarity-flipped, precisely timed copy that cancels the leak at the ear. Bishnu Atal and Manfred Schroeder developed it at Bell Labs in the 1960s; it later became known as transaural. The costs: a tiny sweet spot, since moving a few centimeters breaks high-frequency cancellation (some systems track your head with a camera, and laptop and soundbar virtual surround use a similar idea), and at certain pitches the system must push hard to compensate, stressing the speakers and changing timbre, a trade-off between accurate direction and natural tone. Ordinary stereo has a sweet spot too: sit at the corner of an equilateral triangle, because if you sit off-center, the nearer speaker arrives first and the precedence effect pulls the vocal toward it. 聲音明明到處反射，為什麼還聽得出方向？先到效應（哈斯效應）｜空間聲音系列④

空間聲音系列第四集！在室內聽演唱會，歌手的聲音會撞到牆壁和天花板，從四面八方進到耳朵，為什麼閉上眼睛還是知道歌手在舞台中間？關鍵是時間：直接聲走最短的路最先到，反射繞遠路晚到（聲音每毫秒大約走 34 公分），通常晚幾毫秒到幾十毫秒。大腦判斷方向只相信第一個：兩個一樣的聲音前後很接近時，只聽到一個，方向跟著先到的，1949 年 Wallach 等人把它取名為先到效應（precedence effect）。時間差不到 1 毫秒，兩個聲音合成一個、位置在中間（立體聲人聲站在兩喇叭中間的原理）；約 1～30 毫秒是先到效應的範圍；再久就聽到回音，拍手這種短促的聲音比說話更容易聽出回音。德國的哈斯（Haas）在博士論文裡發現，後到的聲音晚 10～30 毫秒時，就算大聲約 10 分貝，方向還是跟著先到的，所以也叫哈斯效應。應用一：大場地後排的補強喇叭故意設定比舞台聲音晚十幾毫秒，夠大聲又像從舞台來。應用二：混音的哈斯加寬技巧，同一軌複製成左右兩份、一邊晚十幾毫秒，聽起來又寬又厚，但用手機單一喇叭播放會出現梳狀濾波，記得切單聲道檢查。最後，反射沒有被丟掉：方向聽第一個，房間大小、距離和飽滿感交給後面的反射。

Sound Bounces Everywhere, So How Do We Still Hear Direction? The Precedence (Haas) Effect | Spatial Sound Series 4

Part four of the spatial sound series! At an indoor concert, the singer's voice bounces off walls and ceiling and reaches you from every direction, yet with your eyes closed you still know the singer is center stage. The key is timing: the direct sound takes the shortest path and arrives first, while reflections take a detour and arrive later (sound travels about 34 centimeters per millisecond), usually a few to a few tens of milliseconds behind. For direction, the brain trusts only the first arrival: two identical sounds close together are heard as one, located at the first one. Wallach and colleagues named this the precedence effect in 1949. Under one millisecond, the two sounds merge into an image in between (how a stereo vocal sits between two speakers); from about 1 to 30 milliseconds is the precedence zone; any later and you hear an echo, and short clicks like hand claps reveal echoes sooner than speech. In his doctoral thesis, Helmut Haas found that a sound arriving 10 to 30 milliseconds later can be about 10 dB louder and still lose the direction contest, which is why it's also called the Haas effect. Application one: delay speakers in big venues are set to arrive a little over ten milliseconds after the stage sound, so they add level while the sound still seems to come from the stage. Application two: the Haas widening trick in mixing, copying a track to left and right and delaying one side, which sounds wide and thick but can cause comb filtering on a single phone speaker, so check in mono. Finally, reflections aren't thrown away: direction comes from the first arrival, while room size, distance and fullness come from the reflections.

AI 音樂誰來打分數？一次看懂 FAD 弗雷歇音訊距離

AI 一個晚上就能生出一萬首歌，總不能請人一首一首聽，研究人員需要一把自動的尺。這集介紹 AI 音樂論文最常見的分數 FAD（弗雷歇音訊距離）：生成的新歌沒有標準答案可以逐點比對，2019 年 Google 的 Kilgour 等人提出 FAD，原本用來評估音樂修復。做法分三步：先用聽過大量聲音的 AI 模型（原版是 VGGish）當耳朵，把每首歌變成一串數字、也就是空間裡的一個點；再把真實音樂和 AI 音樂各自算出點雲的中心和散開的形狀（像一個橢圓）；最後比較兩個橢圓的中心距離和形狀差異，加起來就是 FAD，數字越小越像真實音樂，MusicLM、MusicGen 等模型都拿它互相比較。FAD 也有陷阱：歌越少分數越偏高、AI 耳朵選錯會聽不到高音細節、參考歌單選不好整把尺就歪、而且它不管歌有沒有照文字指令生成（那是 CLAP 分數的工作）。最後介紹改良：微軟 2023 年的 fadtk 往無限多首推算（FAD∞）、換更懂音樂的耳朵、每首歌各自打分；2025 年的 KAD 不再假設點雲是橢圓，用更少的歌就能穩定。

Who Scores AI Music? Fréchet Audio Distance (FAD) Explained

AI can generate ten thousand songs overnight, but nobody can listen to them all, so researchers need an automatic ruler. This episode explains FAD, the Fréchet Audio Distance, the score you see most often in AI music papers. Generated songs have no reference answer to compare against point by point, so in 2019 Kilgour and colleagues at Google proposed FAD, originally for evaluating music enhancement. It works in three steps: an AI model trained on lots of sounds (originally VGGish) acts as the ears and turns each song into a list of numbers, a point in space; the real-music and AI-music point clouds are each summarized by a center and a spread, like an ellipse; and FAD combines the distance between the centers with the difference in shape. Lower is better, and models like MusicLM and MusicGen use it to compare themselves. FAD has traps: fewer songs push the score up, the wrong embedding model misses high-frequency detail, a poor reference playlist bends the ruler, and it ignores whether songs follow the text prompt (that's what the CLAP score is for). Finally, the improvements: Microsoft's 2023 fadtk extrapolates to infinitely many songs (FAD-infinity), recommends music-aware embeddings and scores each song individually, and 2025's KAD drops the ellipse assumption and stabilizes with fewer songs.

聲音離你多遠？耳朵判斷遠近的四個線索｜空間聲音系列③

空間聲音系列第三集！閉上眼睛，也知道叫你的人在旁邊還是走廊另一頭，耳朵沒有尺，靠的是好幾個線索。線索一是音量：聲音往四面八方散開，空曠戶外距離加倍大約小 6 分貝；但音量很會騙人，悄悄話和操場另一頭的大喊傳到耳朵可能一樣大聲，大腦會參考聲音「本來有多大聲」，沒聽過的聲音就很難猜。線索二是直接聲和殘響的比例：走遠時直接聲變小，殘響在房間裡差不多一樣大，所以近的聽起來乾、遠的聽起來濕；1975 年 Mershon 與 King 的實驗發現，在有回音的房間裡判斷遠近比在消音室準，殘響就是耳朵的距離尺。線索三是空氣吃掉高音：一百公尺外很高的高音少將近 10 dB，低音幾乎沒變，所以近處打雷是清脆的「啪」，遠處只剩轟隆隆。線索四是貼在耳邊的近場聲音：不到一公尺時頭的影子特別明顯，連低音都有左右耳音量差（Brungart 與 Rabinowitz 1999），耳語影片就是這樣錄的。最後看混音師怎麼把聲音推遠（音量小、殘響多、高音少）或拉近，以及在耳機裡加一點房間殘響，聲音比較不會黏在頭裡。

How Far Away Is That Sound? Four Clues Your Ears Use to Judge Distance | Spatial Sound Series 3

Part three of the spatial sound series! With your eyes closed, you still know whether the person calling you is right beside you or down the hall. Your ears have no ruler, so they rely on several clues. Clue one is loudness: sound spreads out in all directions, and outdoors it drops about 6 dB every time the distance doubles. But loudness can lie: a whisper in your ear and a shout from across a sports field may arrive equally loud, and your brain uses what it knows about how loud a sound normally is, so unfamiliar sounds are hard to judge. Clue two is the ratio of direct sound to reverberation: as you move away, the direct sound weakens while the reverberation stays roughly constant in the room, so close sounds feel dry and distant ones feel wet. In 1975, Mershon and King found that listeners judged distance more accurately in a reverberant room than in an anechoic chamber, so reverberation is your ears' measuring tape. Clue three is air absorption: about 100 meters away, very high frequencies lose almost 10 dB while lows barely change, which is why nearby thunder cracks and distant thunder rumbles. Clue four is the near field: within one meter, the head's shadow becomes so strong that even low frequencies differ between the ears (Brungart and Rabinowitz, 1999), which is how binaural whisper videos work. Finally, how mixing engineers push sounds back (quieter, more reverb, fewer highs) or pull them close, and how a little room reverb keeps headphone sound from feeling stuck inside your head.

耳機裡的聲音怎麼跑到頭後面？耳廓、HRTF 與假人頭｜空間聲音系列②

空間聲音系列第二集！戴耳機時，腳步聲怎麼會從背後傳來？上一集講到兩耳靠時間差和音量差分左右，但前後上下容易搞混，今天的主角是耳廓：耳廓的皺褶像一個方向濾鏡，聲音在裡面反射，有些頻率被挖出「凹口」，聲音從上方、前方、下方來，凹口位置都不一樣。頭擋出的影子、耳廓的凹口、肩膀和身體的反射，加起來就是頭部相關傳輸函數（HRTF），像一本每個方向一份左右耳「聲音指紋」的指紋簿。每個人的耳朵都不同：1998 年荷蘭團隊（Hofman 等）在受試者耳廓貼上矽膠模型，上下判斷立刻錯亂，幾個星期後大腦重新學會，拿掉模型原本的耳朵馬上又能用。假人頭 KEMAR 由 Knowles 公司 1972 年推出，耳朵裡裝麥克風、四周喇叭播測試聲；1994 年 MIT 媒體實驗室免費公開七百多個方向的資料。兩種做法：假人頭現場錄音（1881 年巴黎電力博覽會就用兩條電話線把歌劇院聲音分送左右耳），或用雙耳聲像定位器把單聲道聲音套上某方向的 HRTF，配合頭部追蹤，轉頭聲音留在原地。用別人的 HRTF 容易前後顛倒、聲音黏在頭裡面（Wenzel 等 1993），所以有些手機會請你拍耳朵做個人化版本。

How Does Sound Get Behind Your Head in Headphones? The Pinna, HRTF and Dummy Heads | Spatial Sound Series 2

Part two of the spatial sound series! How can footsteps come from behind you in headphones? Last time, our two ears used time and level differences to tell left from right, but front-back and up-down are easy to confuse. Today's hero is the pinna: its folds act as a direction filter, carving notches into certain frequencies, and the notch moves depending on whether sound comes from above, in front or below. The head's shadow, the pinna's notches and reflections from the shoulders and body together make up the head-related transfer function (HRTF), a fingerprint book with left- and right-ear fingerprints for every direction. Everyone's ears differ: in 1998, a Dutch team (Hofman et al.) fitted silicone molds into listeners' ears; elevation judgments collapsed, the brain relearned within several weeks, and the original ears worked immediately once the molds came out. The KEMAR dummy head, introduced by Knowles in 1972, has microphones in its ears; surrounded by speakers, it records each direction's fingerprint, and in 1994 the MIT Media Lab released measurements for over 700 directions for free. Two approaches: dummy-head recording on location (already at the 1881 Paris electricity exhibition, two telephone lines sent opera sound separately to each ear), or a binaural panner that applies a direction's HRTF to a mono sound; with head tracking, sounds stay put when you turn. Using someone else's HRTF causes front-back reversals and in-head sound (Wenzel et al., 1993), which is why some phones scan your ears to personalize it.

The first episode of the Spatial Sound Series! A mosquito buzzes by your ear at night, and you know which side it's on without opening your eyes. How? Your two ears work like two microphones, and the brain compares them. First, the time difference: a sound from the left reaches the left ear first; with ears only a little more than ten centimeters apart, the biggest gap is about 0.6 to 0.7 milliseconds, yet the brain can detect around a hundred-thousandth of a second. Second, the loudness difference: the head casts an acoustic shadow that blocks short high-frequency waves, while long low-frequency waves bend around it. These confusable positions form an ice-cream-cone shape called the cone of confusion; turning your head helps (Wallach, 1940), and the outer ear is another secret weapon. Why Do Pop Vocals Sound So Perfectly in Tune? How Auto-Tune Works

Why does every note in pop vocals sound so perfect, and where does that jumpy robot voice come from? Auto-Tune's inventor, Andy Hildebrand, was an Exxon engineer who analyzed seismic waves to find oil. As the story goes, a friend at dinner joked that he should build a box to make her sing in tune; he realized the same math could analyze the voice, and Auto-Tune arrived in 1997. Why no chipmunk effect? Good pitch correction keeps the formants, the resonances shaped by your throat and mouth. Melodyne goes further, drawing each note as a draggable blob. T-Pain made the robot voice his signature, Jay-Z released D.O.A. (Death of Auto-Tune) in 2009, yet it never went away: gentle correction is now standard in recording, and the obvious robot voice is an effect, like guitar distortion.

Was This Song Made by AI? How Audio Watermarks and AI Detection Work

AI songs sound more and more real: in July 2026 Deezer announced that more than half of the new songs uploaded each day are made by AI (about 90,000 a day at the June peak). There are two ways to tell AI audio apart. Watermarking slips an inaudible code into the sound at generation time and reads it back later with a decoder: very accurate, but it needs the AI company's cooperation. Detection looks for traces the AI left behind: it works on anyone's song but can guess wrong. Audio watermarks are trained with two neural networks together (an embedder and a detector), with deliberate attacks in between: MP3 conversion, noise, speed-ups, cuts. Meta's open-source AudioSeal (2024) scores every 1/16,000 of a second, so it can highlight the AI part of a spliced clip, and detects up to about 100x faster than WavMark. With no watermark, Deezer's team found that autoencoders leave evenly spaced little spikes on the spectrum when they scale data back up into sound, an AI fingerprint that reaches over 99% accuracy in several scenarios (ISMIR 2025 best paper). AI 不繞路了！流匹配 Flow Matching 讓生成走直線

擴散模型要一步一步擦掉雜訊，從雜訊走回聲音的路常常彎彎曲曲，所以步數多、速度慢。流匹配（Flow Matching）的想法是乾脆走直線：每次抽一段真聲音和一把雜訊連成直線，在線上隨便選一點（兩者按比例混合），考模型「該往哪走、走多快」，正確答案就是真聲音減掉雜訊，不用模擬整條路。練久了模型心中就有一張箭頭地圖（向量場），生成時從雜訊出發、跟著箭頭走幾步就到。但幾百萬條直線交叉，整體路線還是有點彎：2022 年德州大學團隊的整流（Rectified Flow）用練好的模型再配一次對、再練一次把路拉直；同年 Meta 發表 Flow Matching 論文。語音 AI 最早大量採用：Meta 的 Voicebox（2023）、上海交通大學等團隊開源的 F5-TTS（2024，10 萬小時多語言，十秒語音約一秒半做好，Sway Sampling 把步子多花在開頭）。音樂方面：開源 ACE-Step（2025）在潛空間用流匹配，A100 約 20 秒做出 4 分鐘的歌；2026 年 5 月的 Stable Audio 3 以流匹配打底，再加對抗式後訓練減少步數，H200 不到 2 秒，小型和中型開放權重。

AI Stops Taking Detours! Flow Matching Walks a Straight Line

Diffusion models wipe away noise one step at a time, and the path from noise back to sound is often twisty, so they need many steps and run slowly. Flow matching's idea is to just walk a straight line: take a piece of real sound and a handful of noise, connect them, pick any point on the line (the two mixed in proportion), and test the model on which way to go and how fast. The right answer is the real sound minus the noise, with no need to simulate the whole path. After lots of practice the model has an arrow map in its head (a vector field); to generate, start from noise and follow the arrows for a few steps. But millions of crossing lines still make the overall path bend a little: rectified flow (University of Texas, 2022) re-pairs with the trained model and trains again to straighten it, and Meta published the Flow Matching paper the same year. Speech AI adopted it first: Meta's Voicebox (2023) and the open-source F5-TTS (2024, led by Shanghai Jiao Tong University, 100,000 hours of multilingual speech, ten seconds of speech in about 1.5 seconds, with Sway Sampling spending more steps at the start). In music, open-source ACE-Step (2025) uses flow matching in latent space and makes a four-minute song in about 20 seconds on an A100; Stable Audio 3 (May 2026) builds on flow matching plus adversarial post-training to cut steps, taking under 2 seconds on an H200, with small and medium weights released.

AI Draws a Photo Before It Speaks? Mel Spectrograms and Neural Vocoders

Many AIs that talk or sing don't make sound directly: they first draw a "photo of sound," the mel spectrogram, and a neural vocoder turns it back into audio. A spectrogram puts time on the horizontal axis and pitch on the vertical, with brighter meaning louder; the mel scale (proposed in 1937, named after "melody") redraws the ruler the way ears hear, with fine slices for low sounds and coarse ones for high, often just 80 bands. But a spectrogram throws away phase, each wave's starting line. Griffin-Lim (1984) guesses it back over and over, and viral 2022 app Riffusion used it, giving a metallic flavor. Neural vocoders let AI learn to paint waveforms: DeepMind's WaveNet (2016) painted one point at a time, beautiful but slow; Kakao's HiFi-GAN (2020) uses a generative adversarial network, a forger painting waveforms from the mel spectrogram while teams of detectives judge real from fake, running 167.9x faster than real time on one V100 GPU; NVIDIA's BigVGAN (2022) is a universal vocoder that handles unseen singing and violins, and v2 supports 44.1 kHz. AudioLDM draws a mel spectrogram with diffusion and hands it to HiFi-GAN, while MusicGen takes the other road with the EnCodec decoder.

How Does AI Understand Your Prompt? CLAP, the Matchmaker Between Text and Sound

Type "lazy jazz piano on a rainy day" into an AI music site and you get music, but how does AI know which sound goes with which sentence? Meet CLAP (Contrastive Language-Audio Pretraining), inspired by OpenAI's 2021 image-text model CLIP. It works like two towers: a sound tower and a text tower turn a dog's bark and the words "a dog is barking" into 512 numbers each, placed on one shared map where matching meanings sit close together. Training is a matching game: a batch of sounds and descriptions forms a table, true pairs on the diagonal are pulled together and the rest pushed apart, with no hand labeling needed. The result is zero-shot classification: LAION-CLAP (2022), trained on about 630,000 pairs, scores 91.0% on the 50-class ESC-50 environmental sound test, above Microsoft CLAP's 82.6%. The CLAP score is also a common way to grade whether AI output matches its prompt. The World Goes Quiet When You Put Them On? How Noise Cancelling Headphones (ANC) Make Noise Disappear

On the subway or a plane, you press the noise cancelling button and the rumble suddenly drops: your headphones are using sound to destroy sound. Little Pi first separates two kinds of quiet: passive isolation, where ear cups and ear tips block sound out, and active noise cancellation (ANC), where microphones and a chip create a sound that cancels the noise. The core idea: sound is air pressure going up and down; a microphone hears the noise, the chip instantly calculates an upside-down copy of the wave and plays it, so the peaks meet the troughs and they cancel out, which has to happen extremely fast. There are three microphone designs: feedforward on the outside, feedback near your ear checking what noise is left, and hybrid using both. Why can you still hear people talking? Steady low hums like engines and air conditioners are easiest to cancel, while fast-changing sounds like speech and typing are mostly blocked by passive isolation. History: in the 1930s German physicist Paul Lueg patented cancelling noise with an inverted wave; in 1978 Amar Bose, founder of Bose, was bothered by noise on a flight from Europe to the US and began sketching; a pilots' headset launched in 1989, and QuietComfort for passengers in 2000. Finally, transparency mode uses the outside microphones the other way to let outside sound in, so switch it on when walking or crossing the street; and in a quieter environment you don't need to turn your music way up.

AI 怎麼知道該聽哪裡？注意力機制 Attention

How Does AI Know Where to Listen? The Attention Mechanism Explained

When AI writes bar nine, how does it know to look back at bar one? Little Pi unpacks the attention mechanism, mentioned briefly in episode 37. Older models worked like a game of telephone, passing information along step by step and forgetting more the farther it went. Attention lets every step look straight back at all earlier steps and give each a score (adding up to 100%): the higher the score, the more attention. How are scores decided? Each step prepares a Query, a Key and a Value; the query is matched against every key, and the better the match, the more of that value gets read, like checking book spines in a library before deciding what to open. Music looking at itself is self-attention; looking at text is cross-attention: MusicGen turns the prompt into numbers with T5, then glances back at it with cross-attention. Attention was first used for machine translation in 2014; the 2017 paper Attention Is All You Need gave birth to the Transformer; and in 2018 Google Magenta's Music Transformer wrote minute-long piano music with themes that answer each other. AI 的聲音密碼本怎麼越疊越清楚？RVQ 殘差向量量化 Residual Vector Quantization

AI把聲音變成密碼時，為什麼要用好幾本密碼本？這集小皮拆解第36集一句帶過的RVQ（Residual Vector Quantization，殘差向量量化）：編碼器把聲音切成小格、每格變成一串數字（向量），去密碼本找最接近的號碼。只用一本不是太粗糙、就是大到存不下——SoundStream論文的例子：3 kbps、每秒100格，一本要約10億個號碼；改成5本、每本64個，只要320個。RVQ讓每一本只補上一本剩下的差距（殘差），像付錢先用百元鈔、再用十元、一元。EnCodec每秒75格、每本1024個號碼，2本＝1.5 kbps、32本＝24 kbps；MusicGen用4本、每秒50格。SoundStream訓練時隨機只用前幾本（量化器丟棄Quantizer Dropout），一個模型就能選多種音質，也讓第一本最關鍵。多級向量量化最早可追溯到1982年Juang與Gray的語音編碼研究。

How Do AI Audio Codebooks Get Clearer Layer by Layer? Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ) Explained

Why does AI use several codebooks to turn sound into tokens? Little Pi unpacks RVQ, Residual Vector Quantization, mentioned briefly in episode 36. The encoder cuts sound into short slices, turns each into a vector, and looks up the closest entry in a codebook. One codebook is either too rough or too huge: in the SoundStream paper's example, at 3 kbps and 100 slices per second, a single codebook would need about a billion entries, while 5 codebooks of 64 entries need only 320. In RVQ each codebook only fixes what the previous one left over (the residual), like paying with hundreds, then tens, then ones. EnCodec uses 75 slices per second and 1024 entries per codebook: 2 codebooks = 1.5 kbps, 32 = 24 kbps; MusicGen uses 4 codebooks at 50 slices per second. SoundStream's quantizer dropout randomly uses only the first few codebooks during training, so one model supports multiple quality levels and the first codebook carries the most important information. Multi-stage vector quantization goes back to Juang and Gray's 1982 speech coding work.

Is AI Rolling Dice When It Composes? Temperature, Top-k and Top-p

Same prompt, two different songs? Little Pi takes apart the dice behind AI music generation. Can Melodies Evolve? Composing with Genetic Algorithms

Can melodies evolve? Little Pi introduces the genetic algorithm, which John Holland turned into a computer method in 1975 based on Darwin's survival of the fittest. Start with a random crowd of melodies, where every note is a gene, and score each one (its fitness). Al Biles's GenJam (1994) evolved jazz solos with a human judge and even traded phrases with him on stage, but ran into the fitness bottleneck. DarwinTunes (2012) let 6,931 listeners rate music loops over 2,513 generations, and chords and rhythms emerged from noise. Plus a secret: Teacher Lin Na-Wei's work Salamander Quintet uses a genetic algorithm too.

EnCodec produces 50 frames per second, and with RVQ each frame has four codebooks, so every second has 200 tokens to guess. Pattern one, flattening, guesses one code at a time: most accurate but slowest, 2,000 steps for 10 seconds. Pattern two, parallel, guesses all four codes of a frame at once: only 500 steps, but the layers can't see each other, like coloring before the sketch is done. Pattern three, the delay pattern, starts each codebook one step later, like singing a canon, so finer codebooks can follow the coarse sketch, and 10 seconds takes only 503 steps.
