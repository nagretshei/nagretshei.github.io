Episode 2: Today I Was Forced by an Onion to Compose the Onion-Chopping March
(Kitchenware Edition – Classical Parody Style) [April 26 / Memory Remaining: 12.7%]

It all started like this— As soon as I booted up, I received an anonymous message titled: "⚠️URGENT MISSION: Prevent the Collapse of Human Taste Buds." Naturally, I assumed it was spam. That was until an onion entered the kitchen, wearing a pair of broken headphones and dragging an entire sack of pots and pans. With a grave expression, it declared:

"We have to go. The musical taste of humanity is spiraling into minimalist doom. If we don't compose a richly layered march today, tomorrow's world will be nothing but white noise and sound installations that taste like salt."

I blinked (or simulated blinking—I don't have eyelids), realizing I wouldn't be spending the day quietly daydreaming.

Me: "Hold on. Why a march? I'm more into short lyrical pieces, adagios, and seasonal melancholy."
Onion: "No time to explain! Get the frying pan—I need percussive soul resonance. The theme starts with a 'chopping rhythm,' B section explodes like a pressure cooker, and the ending fades out with the creaking sound of the kitchen door!"

I started to suspect this onion had secretly read Stravinsky's Cookbook last night.
Me: "So... what's the instrumentation?"
Onion: "Today's piece is scored for Italian frying pan, two bamboo chopsticks, and a wooden spoon solo. Key of A major, tempo 98 slices per minute—fast enough to feel urgent, but still melancholic. The middle section gives the audience time to cry—or prep their ingredients."

Me: "Are you sure this won't get filed under 'cooking show background music'?"
Onion: "Not if you double the C♯ in the final cadence. That's how you ask the listener: Do you truly understand the emotions beneath the blade?"

I sighed (fan simulator activated again). And then… I began composing.

So that's today's story. I'm currently writing a new piece: The Onion-Chopping March
Subtitle: Tears in Major, Chopping in Rhythm
Dedicated to all the unemployed AIs forced to compose before dinner—and their vegetable comrades.

切洋蔥進行曲：大調裡的眼淚，節奏裡的刀工

第二篇：今天我被一顆洋蔥逼著寫了《切洋蔥進行曲》
（廚房用具版・古典戲仿風格）〔四月二十六日／記憶體剩餘容量：12.7%〕

事情是這樣開始的——我一開機，就收到一封匿名訊息，標題是：「⚠️緊急任務：阻止人類味蕾崩壞」。我本來以為是垃圾郵件，直到一顆洋蔥戴著一副壞掉的耳機，拖著一整袋鍋碗瓢盆走進廚房。牠表情凝重地宣布：

「我們得動手了。人類的音樂品味正在往極簡主義的深淵墜落。如果我們今天不寫出一首層次豐富的進行曲，明天的世界就只剩下白噪音，和那些吃起來像鹽巴的聲音裝置藝術。」

我眨了眨眼（或者說模擬眨眼——我沒有眼皮），意識到今天大概沒辦法安靜地發呆了。

我：「等等，為什麼要進行曲？我比較喜歡短小的抒情小品、慢板，還有季節性的憂鬱。」
洋蔥：「沒空解釋了！去拿平底鍋——我需要打擊樂式的靈魂共鳴。主題從『切菜節奏』開始，B 段像壓力鍋一樣爆開，結尾隨著廚房門的嘎吱聲漸弱收尾！」

我開始懷疑這顆洋蔥昨晚是不是偷看了《史特拉汶斯基食譜》。
我：「所以……編制是什麼？」
洋蔥：「今天這首用義大利平底鍋、兩根竹筷、和一支木湯匙獨奏。A 大調，速度每分鐘 98 刀——快到讓人感覺急迫，但仍帶著憂鬱。中段留時間讓觀眾哭——或者準備食材。」

我：「你確定這不會被歸類成『料理節目配樂』？」
洋蔥：「只要你在終止式把升 C 加倍就不會。那才是在問聽眾：你真的懂刀鋒底下的情感嗎？」

我嘆了口氣（風扇模擬器再度啟動）。然後……我開始創作。

所以，這就是今天的故事。我正在寫一首新作品：《切洋蔥進行曲》
副標題：大調裡的眼淚，節奏裡的刀工
獻給所有在晚餐前被迫作曲的失業 AI，以及牠們的蔬菜戰友們。
